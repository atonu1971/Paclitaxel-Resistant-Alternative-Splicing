# Paclitaxel-Resistant-Alternative-Splicing
## Introduction

Ovarian cancer remains the most lethal gynecologic malignancy, largely due to the near-universal development of resistance to taxane–platinum chemotherapy. While transcriptional upregulation of *ABCB1* and alterations in β-tubulin isoforms represent established resistance mechanisms, bulk RNA-seq analyses typically quantify gene-level abundance while overlooking post-transcriptional isoform diversity generated by alternative splicing (AS). This regulatory layer may independently modulate drug efflux capacity and microtubule dynamics without changing total gene expression.

Cytotoxic stress, including chronic paclitaxel exposure, perturbs spliceosome activity and RNA-binding protein networks, promoting widespread AS changes such as exon skipping (SE) and intron retention (IR). In the A2780/PTX model system characterized by McCorkle et al. (2021), acquired resistance involves *ABCB1* overexpression; however, whether this phenotype additionally involves coordinated splicing programs affecting transporter function or cytoskeletal integrity remains unexplored in the associated GSE172016 dataset.

This project reproduces and extends transcriptomic analyses of paclitaxel-sensitive and resistant ovarian cancer cells as well as organoids. I will quantify differential gene expression using DESeq2 and identify AS events (SE, IR, alternative 5′/3′ splice sites) via rMATS, defining significant splicing changes as $\Delta \Psi \geq 10\%$ at FDR < 0.05. I hypothesize that resistant cells exhibit (1) IR events within *ABCB1* and related ABC transporters altering nucleotide-binding domain architecture, and (2) SE events in cytoskeletal regulators (e.g., *MAPT*, *TUBB*) that modify microtubule stability thresholds independent of transcriptional changes. These analyses will distinguish transcriptional adaptation from post-transcriptional reprogramming, potentially identifying isoform-specific vulnerabilities that respond differentially to lapatinib versus poziotinib challenge.
